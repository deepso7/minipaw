---
title: What minipaw is
description: minipaw pipes stdin/stdout between two machines, peer to peer. Like netcat, but it gets through NATs, needs no accounts, and every byte is end-to-end encrypted.
---

minipaw is a pipe between two machines. Whatever goes into one side's stdin
comes out of the other side's stdout, so anything that reads or writes a
stream works over it: a chat, a file, a tar of a folder, an SSH session.

## How the pieces fit

- **A ticket** is the whole address. The listening side prints it, and the
  other side pastes it. It carries the listener's public key and a random
  token, so whoever holds it can connect: share it like a password.
  [Tickets](/docs/tickets)
- **A relay** gets the two sides talking first, wherever they are, and only
  ever sees ciphertext.
- **A direct path** replaces the relay when hole punching gets through both
  networks, mid-session and without losing a byte. When it can't, traffic
  stays on the relay. [How it works](/docs/how-it-works)

There's no account and no control plane. minipaw is built on
[minip2p](https://github.com/deepso7/minip2p), a small libp2p implementation
in Rust, and its networking is also a [Rust library](/docs/library) you can
embed.
